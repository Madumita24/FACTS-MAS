## Summary

1. **One single model is the best method at every horizon: factor-adjusted damped ETS (FA-ETS-d).** It divides by the window-7 seasonal factors, fits a damped-trend ETS, and multiplies back. It scores **1.974 / 3.608 / 5.664** against R1c's 2.122 / 4.468 / 6.987. Validation picks it in every fold at 8 and 13 weeks. It beats R1c in 5 / 6 / 6 of 6 folds and in 12 / 15 / 15 of 15 MSAs. A truncation test and a negative control show it uses no future data.
2. **Main question, answered plainly: the production FACTS-MAS (R1c) does not beat the best validation-selected single model at any horizon** (2.122 vs 2.012, 4.468 vs 3.608, 6.987 vs 5.664). The only FACTS-MAS variants that match it (C2, C3) give that same model 92-99% of the weight. They beat it slightly at 4 weeks (1.979 vs 2.012; better in 4 of 6 folds) and lose slightly at 8 and 13 weeks (3 of 6 folds each). Fusion adds nothing measurable beyond the best single model.
3. **Promotion rule: C2 is promoted at 8 and 13 weeks** (it wins on validation and on test in 6 of 6 folds). No candidate qualifies at 4 weeks, where all fail the validation condition. The rule's outcome is "R1c at 4 weeks, C2 at 8 and 13 weeks": **2.122 / 3.622 / 5.735**. In practice, C2 is FA-ETS-d with a little seasonal weight.
4. **Horizon-only weights (no Router) match R1c at 8 weeks and beat it at 13 weeks** (4.468 vs 4.468; 6.826 vs 6.987). This is more evidence that the Router's value is horizon conditioning, not regime conditioning.
5. **On Zillow's raw (unsmoothed) series every method gets worse, and the advantage over naive shrinks sharply.** R1c's 4-week reduction vs naive falls from 49% to 24%. Holt is worse than naive at 8 and 13 weeks, and R1c is worse than the standalone seasonal channel at 4 and 13 weeks. FA-ETS-d is still best at every horizon.

## What surprised me and how I checked it

- **FA-ETS-d's margin** (1.3 MAPE points at 13 weeks over R1c, against my prediction of within 0.3). A surprisingly good result gets extra scrutiny, so I checked:
  - leakage: truncation test, negative control, and parity with stored output, all passing;
  - consistency: validation picks it, and it wins in every fold and every MSA at 8 and 13 weeks;
  - robustness: it is still best on the raw cut, with a smaller margin.

  The mechanism is plausible. A linear blend of a non-seasonal trend forecast and a seasonal-ratio forecast is not the same as fitting a trend to the deseasonalised series. Holt on the raw seasonal series mistakes seasonal swings for trend, and the damping stops that trend from running away. Phase 4 still has to test it statistically.
- **The raw cut halves the 4-week gain.** Zillow's smoothed weekly series is believed to be a trailing average. That makes it smoother and easier to extrapolate than real listings, and the 4-week headline benefits most. I checked that the smoothed vintage is identical to July, so this is not a revision effect.
- **Horizon-only weights beat the Router at 13 weeks.** This is consistent with Phase 2, but the per-fold record is mixed (3 of 6), so I treat it as supporting evidence, not proof.
- Six of my twelve expectations were wrong or partly wrong; they are listed in `phase3_log.md`.

## Things I could not determine

- Whether the FA-ETS-d advantage is statistically significant once correlation across MSAs and origins is accounted for. That is Phase 4.
- How Zillow builds its smoothed series. My reading that it is a trailing average comes from their methodology notes and is not confirmed.

## Earlier conclusions I now revise

- **Phase 2 C17 ("temporal and seasonal channels exchange roles across horizons", marked holds):** revised to weakened. The handoff exists only because the temporal channel ignores seasonality.
- **Phase 2's reading that the Router's 13-week value is horizon conditioning** was "medium, pending Phase 4". It now has a second piece of evidence: horizon-only weights do at least as well pooled. Still pending the Phase 4 placebo.
- **Phase 0-2 headline framing:** the size of the gain over naive depends heavily on using the smoothed series (F3.8). This was flagged as minor in F0.13 and is now major.
